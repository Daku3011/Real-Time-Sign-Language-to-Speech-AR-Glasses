# 🗣️ Slide-by-Slide Script

## 🎬 ACT 1: The Vision & The Problem (Dwarkesh - 3 mins)

### Slide 1: Introduction (1 min)
**[Dwarkesh]** 
"Good morning, respected evaluation panel. We are Team Inno8 from SCET Surat, and today we are presenting our Real-Time Sign Language to Speech AR Glasses. 

Simply put, we are building affordable, wearable technology to break the communication barrier for the deaf community. Our mission is to give a voice back to the millions of Indians who deserve to be heard, by blending embedded systems with on-device edge AI."

### Slide 2: Business Overview (1 min)
**[Dwarkesh]** 
"To understand our business, you must first understand the massive gap. Today, 63 million Indians with hearing loss face profound isolation. Why? Because there are only 340 certified sign language interpreters in the entire country—a staggering ratio of 1 to 185,000.

The impact is tragic: 67% cannot communicate independently in hospitals, and 74% are blocked from career advancement. We are solving this massive unaddressed need by replacing the scarce human interpreter with a reliable, offline, wearable AI."

### Slide 10: Financial Analysis (1 min)
**[Dwarkesh]**
"Financially, our model is highly scalable. While initial prototypes cost ₹22,000, achieving a 10,000-unit mass production run drops our unit cost to exactly ₹8,206. 

By selling at the ADIP-capped retail price of ₹15,000, we maintain a highly lucrative 45% margin. Our projections show us breaking even at just 500 units, scaling to a ₹15 Crore run-rate in Year 3 as we hit national distribution."

*(Dwarkesh passes the floor)*
**[Dwarkesh]** "Now, I'll pass the floor to Drashtant to discuss how we plan to reach this market."

## 📈 ACT 2: Strategy & Market Scope (Drashtant - 3 mins)

### Slide 3: Strategy & Go-To-Market (1.5 mins)
**[Drashtant]**
"Thank you, Dwarkesh. I'm Drashtant, and I will outline our market entry strategy, which relies on a distinct three-phase approach.

**Phase 1** is B2G via the ADIP Scheme. The Indian government subsidizes assistive tech up to 100% for BPL users. By securing empanelment, our device becomes absolutely free for the end user, while guaranteeing us high-volume government procurement.
**Phase 2** is B2C Direct Sales, focusing on urban professionals and students who are willing to self-fund a ₹15,000 device for their career and academic growth.
**Phase 3** is B2B Corporate CSR. We will sell directly to inclusive employers, like Amazon or Lemon Tree Hotels, equipping their hearing-impaired workforce to thrive."

### Slide 5: Market Opportunity (1.5 mins)
**[Drashtant]**
"The market opportunity here is massive. While the Total Addressable Market is 63 million people with hearing loss, our Serviceable Reachable Market is 1.9 Lakh disabled individuals in Gujarat alone. Our Year 1 Serviceable Obtainable Market is a conservative 50,000 early adopters.

The Indian Assistive Tech market is booming towards $2.5 Billion, growing at a strong CAGR. Most importantly, major tech companies like Meta and Google have ignored this specific niche, as it lacks multi-billion dollar smartphone-like margins. This leaves a massive blue-ocean opportunity for a localized, highly-specialized startup like ours."

*(Drashtant passes to Akifa)*
**[Drashtant]** "Akifa will now delve deeper into our exact targets and competitive landscape."

## ⚔️ ACT 3: Competition & Targets (Akifa - 3 mins)

### Slide 4: Competitive Analysis (1.5 mins)
**[Akifa]**
"Thank you, Drashtant. I am Akifa, and I will discuss our competitive positioning.

When looking at alternatives, the landscape is deeply flawed. Smartphone apps are cheap but occupy both hands, ruining natural sign language. Human interpreters are ideal but cost over ₹30,000 monthly. Competitor AR glasses like XRAI Glass cost upwards of ₹40,000 and only provide speech-to-text captions, entirely ignoring the deaf person's ability to sign back.

Our 'unfair advantage' is clear: We are truly hands-free, completely private via offline processing, natively built for Indian Sign Language, and priced strategically at ₹15,000 to perfectly qualify for 100% government ADIP subsidies."

### Slide 8: Target Segments & Regulatory Drivers (1 min)
**[Akifa]**
"We are precisely targeting four segments: Deaf students needing educational access, working professionals pushing for career growth, hospitals requiring critical misdiagnosis protection, and rural populations reliant on subsidies. 

We also have a massive regulatory tailwind: The RPwD Act of 2016 explicitly mandates barrier-free accessibility in government and public institutions. We provide the exact, scalable hardware required for these institutions to legally comply."

### Slide 12: Exit Strategy (0.5 mins)
**[Akifa]**
"Finally, how do investors realize value? We project three exit pathways: Acquisition by MedTech giants like Cochlear looking to enter the Indian market, licensing our hyper-efficient ISL engine to OEMs like Meta, or operating as a highly profitable, sustainable social enterprise fueled by CSR. 

Join us in bringing a voice to the silent."

*(Akifa passes to Jenny)*
**[Akifa]** "I'll now invite Jenny to walk you through the experience."

## ✨ ACT 4: Experience & Execution (Jenny - 3 mins)

### Slide 6: Product Overview / UX (1.5 mins)
**[Jenny]**
"Thank you, Akifa. I'm Jenny, and I focus on the User Experience. 

How does the device actually feel to use? The experience is built on a seamless, 3-step natural interaction. First, the deaf user simply wears the lightweight glasses and signs naturally with both hands free. Second, the built-in camera processes the gestures entirely offline ensuring 100% privacy. Third, the translation is instantly spoken aloud via bone-conduction audio transducers, allowing the hearing person to understand immediately. 

In our validation interviews with deaf students in Surat, 80% expressed daily usage intent because of this frictionless, natural design."

### Slide 9: Organisation & Team (0.5 mins)
**[Jenny]**
"Executing this requires a specialized team. Dwarkesh and Jenil drive the AI and Hardware, I handle the UI and UX, while Akifa and Drashtant drive our GTM and market research. Mentored by Prof. Pariza Kamboj, we possess the exact multidisciplinary mix needed to bring this to production."

This funding completes our transition from desktop software to standalone wearable prototypes. 33% is allocated for securing our IP and filing patents, 30% for dedicated edge-hardware like micro-displays and compute modules, and the remainder for 3D casting and field testing. This small seed ensures we lock down our intellectual property and get units onto the faces of 20 test users."

## 🛠️ ACT 5: The Tech & Live Demo (Jenil - 3 mins)

### Slide 7: Product Deep Dive & Live Demo (3 mins)
**[Jenil]**
"I'm Jenil, the Hardware Lead. Let's look under the hood. 

Our architecture leverages a Raspberry Pi Zero W capturing 30 frames per second. We use MediaPipe to track 21 3D landmarks per hand. These coordinates feed into our custom TensorFlow Lite CNN model which classifies the sequence and pushes it to a localized text-to-speech synthesizer. All of this happens natively on the 80-gram wearable with sub-500 millisecond latency.

*(Action: Jenil steps forward with the prototype/laptop)*
Now, I'd like to demonstrate this live...
*(Conduct 1-1.5 minute Live Demo: Perform 3 to 4 ISL gestures: "Hello", "Need", "Help", "Thank You". System outputs the audio).*

As you can see, the recognition is robust and the tactile response is instantaneous, currently achieving an 85% accuracy rate on our MVP vocabulary. Thank you everyone for your time."

# 💡 Setup Tips for the Team:
- **Pacing:** Practice your sections with a timer. You each have exactly 3 minutes. Do not rush.
- **Transitions:** Notice how each person introduces the next speaker or clearly hands off the mic (e.g., "Thank you, Drashtant"). This looks incredibly professional to judges.
- **The Demo:** Keep the live demo short and flawless. Test the lighting in the presentation room beforehand to make sure the hand tracking works perfectly.
